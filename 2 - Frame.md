# Process

The consultant reviews the **Idea** document and structures the available information using the Product Framing board.

The analysis follows a general sequence:

1. identify the relevant Customer Segments
2. define their core Jobs to Be Done
3. formulate the initial Value Proposition
4. capture the expected Revenue Streams
5. review relevant Competitors and Alternatives
6. divide the expected product scope into MVP and later Product Stages
7. identify known or expected Integrations
8. capture Constraints that may affect the product or its implementation

Information should only be added when it can be reasonably derived from the Idea document or supported by additional research.

Missing information should not be invented.

Any assumptions, contradictions, or unanswered questions discovered during Product Framing should be captured for validation.

---

# Validation

The completed Product Framing board is reviewed with the client or relevant decision-makers.

The purpose of validation is to:

- confirm that the product context has been understood correctly
- validate the identified Customer Segments and Jobs to Be Done
- validate the proposed Value Proposition
- review the expected Revenue Streams
- confirm the understanding of Competitors and Alternatives
- review the proposed Product Stages / Scope
- confirm known Integrations and Constraints
- resolve critical assumptions, contradictions, and open questions

Validation may result in changes to the Product Framing board.

The consultant updates the product framing based on the validation results before preparing the final stage document.

---

# Output

The stage produces a **Frame** document.

The document contains:

- Customer Segments
- Jobs to Be Done
- Value Proposition
- Revenue Streams
- Competitors / Alternatives
- Product Stages / Scope
- Integrations
- Constraints
- remaining assumptions and open questions

The document is prepared based on the validated Product Framing board and represents the agreed product and business context for the next stage.

---

# Success Criteria

The stage is complete when:

- the target Customer Segments are identified
- the core Jobs to Be Done are understood
- the expected Value Proposition is defined
- the monetization hypothesis is captured, where relevant
- relevant Competitors and Alternatives are understood
- the product scope is divided into appropriate Product Stages
- known Integrations and Constraints are captured
- the Product Framing board has been reviewed with the client
- critical misunderstandings and contradictions have been resolved
- the validated results are captured in the Frame document

Once these conditions are satisfied, the process can move to the next stage: **Structure**.

## Failure Conditions

The stage is considered unsuccessful if the consultant cannot establish a coherent product framing based on the available information.

This may include situations where:

- the target customer or their core job remains unclear
- the expected product value cannot be articulated
- the proposed scope cannot be reasonably defined
- critical constraints or dependencies remain unknown
- major contradictions remain unresolved after validation
- the client does not confirm the fundamental product hypothesis

In such cases, the consultant should return to the unresolved areas, request additional information, or revisit the **Idea** stage where necessary.
