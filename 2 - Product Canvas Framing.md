# LEKAL 2 — Product Framing

The goal of this stage is to transform the collected idea context into a structured product and business hypothesis before moving into detailed product design.

At this point, the initial idea has already been clarified and its context has been captured during LEKAL 1. However, the product still exists mostly as a collection of intentions, assumptions, and expectations.

The consultant now structures this information into a coherent view of the product: who it is for, what value it is expected to create, how it fits into the existing market, how its scope may evolve over time, and which external constraints may affect it.

Product Framing is performed by the consultant as a preparation step. The resulting product hypothesis will be validated and refined with the client during the next stage.

---

# Objective

Build an initial structured view of the product and its business context based on the information collected during LEKAL 1.

The objective of this stage is to establish:

- who the product is intended for
- what job or problem the product addresses
- what value the product is expected to create
- how the product is expected to generate revenue
- how the same problem is currently solved
- how the product scope can be divided between MVP and later stages
- which external systems the product may need to interact with
- which constraints may affect the product or its implementation

The result is an initial product hypothesis that can be reviewed, challenged, and refined with the client.

---

# Input

The stage starts with the **Idea Context Snapshot** produced during LEKAL 1.

The snapshot contains the information collected from the initial idea, including known context, expectations, existing assumptions, and identified uncertainties.

At this stage, the consultant does not attempt to design the product in detail.

Instead, the available information is analyzed and structured into an initial product hypothesis.

---

# Focus Areas

During this stage, the consultant analyzes the product across several connected areas.

### Customer Segments

Who is the product intended for?

Identify the main customer and user groups that may interact with, purchase, or benefit from the product.

Consider:

- primary users
- secondary users
- customers and buyers
- differences between customer segments where relevant

The goal is not to define detailed personas, but to understand who the product needs to create value for.

### Jobs to Be Done

What is the customer trying to accomplish?

Identify the core job or jobs for which the customer may use the product.

Focus on:

- what the customer is trying to achieve
- what problem or situation creates the need
- what outcome the customer expects

The goal is to describe the need independently from a specific implementation or feature set.

### Value Proposition

Why should the customer use this product?

Define the expected value the product creates for its target customers and users.

Consider:

- what problem the product solves
- what becomes easier, faster, cheaper, safer, or otherwise better
- why the proposed product may be preferable to existing approaches

The value proposition should connect directly to the identified customer segments and their Jobs to Be Done.

### Revenue Streams

How is the product expected to generate revenue?

Capture the current monetization hypothesis, if one exists.

Consider:

- who pays
- what they pay for
- how payment may work
- whether different customer segments may have different revenue models

A detailed pricing model is not required at this stage.

### Competitors / Alternatives

How is the same problem solved today?

Identify direct competitors as well as alternative ways customers currently perform the same job.

Consider:

- direct competing products
- indirect competitors
- manual or internal solutions
- existing tools used as substitutes
- relevant differences between the proposed product and existing alternatives

The purpose is not to perform a complete market analysis, but to understand the environment in which the product will exist.

### Product Stages / Scope

How can the product evolve over time?

Structure the currently known product scope into logical stages.

For example:

- MVP
- next stage
- future development

The MVP should contain only what is necessary to deliver the core product value and test the initial product hypothesis.

Ideas that are useful but not necessary for the first version should be explicitly placed into later stages rather than mixed into the MVP.

At this stage, the scope remains a hypothesis and may change after validation with the client.

### Integrations

Which external systems or services may the product need to interact with?

Identify known or expected integrations that may affect the product scope.

Consider:

- external platforms
- third-party services
- APIs
- authentication providers
- payment providers
- existing client systems
- other external dependencies

Detailed integration design is outside the scope of this stage.

The goal is to identify integrations early enough to understand their potential impact on the product.

### Constraints

What limits or influences the possible solution?

Capture known constraints that may affect product scope, implementation, or delivery.

Consider:

- deadlines
- budget limitations
- technical or platform constraints
- regulatory or legal requirements
- existing infrastructure
- mandatory technologies or systems
- organizational or operational constraints

Constraints should be treated as inputs into product decisions rather than problems to solve during this stage.

---

# Process

The consultant reviews the Idea Context Snapshot and structures the available information using the Product Framing board.

The analysis follows a general sequence:

1. identify the relevant Customer Segments
2. define their core Jobs to Be Done
3. formulate the initial Value Proposition
4. capture the expected Revenue Streams
5. review relevant Competitors and Alternatives
6. divide the expected product scope into MVP and later Product Stages
7. identify known or expected Integrations
8. capture Constraints that may affect the product or its implementation

Information should only be added when it can be reasonably derived from the Idea Context Snapshot or supported by additional research.

Missing information should not be invented.

Any assumptions, contradictions, or unanswered questions discovered during Product Framing should be captured for validation with the client during the next stage.

---

# Output

The output of this stage is a completed **Product Framing Board** containing:

- Customer Segments
- Jobs to Be Done
- Value Proposition
- Revenue Streams
- Competitors / Alternatives
- Product Stages / Scope
- Integrations
- Constraints
- assumptions and open questions identified during the analysis

The Product Framing Board represents the consultant's initial product hypothesis.

It is not considered a validated product definition.

Its purpose is to provide a structured foundation for the next stage, where the hypothesis can be reviewed and refined together with the client.
