# Process

The consultant starts with the validated **Shape** document and transforms the selected product scope into a structured product model.

The process follows a general sequence:

1. build the High-Level Product Flow for the selected product scope
2. identify logical Functional Areas based on the flow
3. map known capabilities to the appropriate Functional Areas
4. prepare a visual concept for each Functional Area
5. review the resulting structure for gaps, overlaps, and inconsistencies
6. capture assumptions and open questions discovered during the analysis

The product structure should remain consistent with the scope, integrations, and constraints established during Product Framing.

New functionality should not be introduced solely to complete the structure.

If additional functionality appears necessary for the product flow to work, it should be explicitly identified and validated before being included in the product structure.

---

# Validation

The High-Level Product Flow, Functional Areas, and Functional Area Concepts are reviewed with the client or relevant decision-makers.

The purpose of validation is to:

- confirm that the High-Level Product Flow correctly represents how the product is expected to work
- confirm that the identified Functional Areas represent meaningful parts of the product
- validate the responsibilities and boundaries of each Functional Area
- validate the capabilities assigned to each Functional Area
- identify missing functionality or interactions
- resolve critical assumptions, contradictions, and open questions

Validation may result in changes to the Product Flow, Functional Areas, Functional Area Concepts, or previously defined product scope.

The consultant updates the visual product structure based on the validation results before preparing the final stage document.

---

# Output

The stage produces a **Structure** document.

The document contains:

- High-Level Product Flow
- Functional Areas
- capabilities mapped to Functional Areas
- Functional Area Concepts
- connections between Functional Areas
- remaining assumptions and open questions

The document is prepared based on the validated product structure and represents the agreed high-level structure of the product.

It provides the foundation for detailed decomposition into scenarios, rules, and requirements during the following stage.

---

# Success Criteria

The stage is complete when:

- the High-Level Product Flow describes how the selected product scope works end-to-end
- the major Functional Areas are identified
- known capabilities are mapped to the appropriate Functional Areas
- a concept is defined for each Functional Area
- responsibilities and boundaries of the Functional Areas are understood
- key connections between Functional Areas are identified
- the product structure has been reviewed with the client
- critical gaps, contradictions, and misunderstandings have been resolved
- the validated results are captured in the Structure document

Once these conditions are satisfied, the process can move to the next stage: **Requirements**.

## Failure Conditions

The stage is considered unsuccessful if the consultant cannot establish a coherent product structure based on the validated **Shape**.

This may include situations where:

- the product cannot be represented as a coherent end-to-end flow
- major parts of the product cannot be assigned to clear Functional Areas
- the responsibilities or boundaries of Functional Areas remain unclear
- critical functionality required for the product flow is missing from the agreed scope
- major contradictions remain unresolved after validation
- the client does not confirm the fundamental product structure

In such cases, the consultant should return to the unresolved areas, revise the product structure, or revisit the **Shape** stage where changes to the product framing or scope are required.
